==================================================
PRIMARY QUESTION
==================================================

Determine whether the current TroubleshootJS architecture can realistically evolve from today's small circuit families into believable complex circuit boards without degrading into random collections of components.

The long-term target is not merely:

"generate more resistors and capacitors."

The target is eventually being able to create coherent boards resembling the electrical complexity of things such as:

- basic coffee makers;
- small appliance controllers;
- pump controllers;
- HVAC/control boards;
- simple power supplies;
- motor/fan controllers;
- timer-based equipment;
- sensor/control/output systems.

These boards should contain multiple functional subsystems that interact coherently.

The generator must eventually create circuits that make electrical sense.

==================================================
CORE CONCERN
==================================================

The central risk to investigate is:

As board complexity increases, will procedural generation naturally produce believable functional circuitry, or will it tend to produce nonsense such as:

- arbitrary resistors added only for variety;
- random capacitors with no electrical purpose;
- LEDs inserted without a meaningful role;
- redundant pull-ups/pull-downs;
- meaningless parallel paths;
- components whose values are electrically possible but functionally absurd;
- circuits that technically solve but do not represent any plausible device;
- faults with no meaningful symptom;
- red-herring circuitry that is obviously random;
- unsolvable or ambiguous challenges.

Do not assume more components automatically means more realism.

11. AUTOMATIC SOLVABILITY
==================================================

This is another major focus.

A generated board should not merely be electrically valid.

It must be troubleshootable.

Investigate what automated validation may eventually be required to reject bad challenges.

Possible criteria:

- healthy board reaches expected operating behavior;
- fault produces a customer-visible symptom;
- fault does not make simulation unstable;
- board remains repairable;
- at least one valid repair exists;
- correct repair restores behavior;
- fault is distinguishable through accessible measurements;
- required diagnostic nodes are physically probeable;
- player is not required to know hidden metadata;
- no impossible measurement sequence is required;
- normal repair actions can actually isolate the fault.

Determine which of these can be realistically automated and which may require curated block metadata.

12. DIAGNOSTIC DISTINGUISHABILITY
==================================================

Complex boards may contain several faults that produce similar symptoms.

Investigate how generation can avoid ambiguous or trivial challenges.

For example:

Customer complaint:
"Motor does not run."

Possible causes:
- missing supply;
- blown fuse;
- failed MOSFET;
- open motor connection;
- missing gate drive.

Those can be excellent alternatives if measurements distinguish them.

But if two generated faults produce exactly the same accessible observations, the player may have no rational way to choose.

Discuss possible future validation strategies such as:

- diagnostic signatures;
- accessible measurement vectors;
- scenario compatibility metadata;
- curated fault groups.
